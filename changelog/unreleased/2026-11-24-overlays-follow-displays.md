- Overlays can follow the displays while an app runs, so a desktop shell keeps one panel per monitor. `App::on_displays_changed(|displays, cx| ..)` runs on the event thread at startup with every connected display and, on Wayland, again whenever one is plugged in, unplugged or changes mode, scale, position or name, whether or not the app has a window or any overlay (other platforms call it at startup only; headless runs and the web never do). On Wayland `Display::name` there is the connector's (`DP-2`), and a new display is listed once the compositor has described it. `DisplaysCx::overlay(spec, state)` adds an overlay from the handler and `AppHandle::add_overlay(|| spec, state)` from any thread, both like `App::overlay` (the app's fonts, font smoothing and `poll`, `setup`, prewarm), and `OverlayHandle::remove()` takes an overlay away for good: its surface, renderer, runtime and state go on the next turn, and later calls on its handles do nothing. A Wayland overlay on a display of its own (`DisplayTarget::Id`, or `Native` with a Wayland id) no longer moves to whichever display the compositor picks once its own is gone, where it reserved that display's edge a second time: when the compositor closes its surface it hides and `availability()` says `Unavailable` (`failure()` says why), and a later `show` fails the same way while that display is missing, so the app can remove it. Any Wayland overlay whose surface the compositor closed is `Unavailable` until a later `show` makes a new one; before, it was only hidden. `examples/layer-bar` now keeps a bar on every display and says on stderr when it adds or removes one.

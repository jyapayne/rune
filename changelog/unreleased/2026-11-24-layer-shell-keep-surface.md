- Wayland layer-shell overlays: a `prewarm` overlay keeps its surface across hide and show, and no show waits for the compositor on the event thread. A hide unmaps the layer surface with a null buffer instead of destroying it, and the next show commits its role state again (size, anchors, margins, exclusive zone, keyboard interactivity, input region, buffer scale) and draws when the compositor's `configure` arrives, on the same `wl_surface`, layer surface and swapchain; focus, input method, key repeat, the pointer over it and its frame callback are let go at the unmap. An overlay that isn't prewarmed, or that gives its GPU back (`release_gpu_when_hidden`, `release_gpu_after`), is still made for each show. Making a layer surface no longer blocks for up to two seconds on its first `configure` either: the first frame draws when it arrives, and a surface the compositor doesn't configure within two seconds hides its overlay, which reports `Unavailable` with a `failure()` reason rather than being refused at creation (it no longer falls back to XWayland then; a compositor without layer-shell still does).
